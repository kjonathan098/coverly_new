Coverly: three visual directions

Newer: Phase 2B homepage prototype (Electric Pop, desktop and mobile) with its design language, and the earlier cover color study.

Every concept uses the same fictional content: one original, five covers and four stories. The differences you see come from the design, not the copy. Previews are scaled-down live pages; open a concept on its own to judge type and color at full size.

A. Pop Editorial

Every issue is a magazine cover.

Open concept A

Strengths

  • Most familiar reading pattern; scales naturally into a real magazine with issues and features
  • Color-flooded blocks make content types easy to tell apart
  • The chronological version list is the clearest way to compare covers

Risks

  • Leans on strong imagery; without photography or commissioned art it can feel flat
  • Closest of the three to existing pop-culture magazines

B. Type Explosion

Every section is a gig poster; the type is the image.

Open concept B

Strengths

  • Most distinctive; needs almost no imagery, so it works with today's data
  • “Same song, new letters” turns the core feature into the visual idea
  • Cheapest to build and fastest to load

Risks

  • Each cover needs its own lettering treatment, which doesn't scale to thousands automatically
  • Very large condensed type can slow scanning, and the hero takes a full screen

C. Cut & Paste

A record-store wall, pinned over a visible cutting-mat grid.

Open concept C

Strengths

  • Warmest and most playful; community contributions would fit naturally as “pinned” items
  • The family tree shows the original↔cover relationship most literally
  • Each object type (cassette, ticket, polaroid) can encode a version's character

Risks

  • Rotations, textures and three typefaces cost readability, especially the typewriter body text
  • Collage layouts are the hardest to keep tidy as content grows, and the hardest to build
