Coverly

Design language: Electric Pop

A proposal drawn from the Phase 2B homepage prototype. Values here are starting points for the production design system, not final tokens. See the prototype.

Principles

  • Reading happens on paper; listening happens on ink. Stories and long-form sit on warm paper. Covers, players and exploration sit on ink.
  • Every version has a color. The original is always hot pink. Covers take the turquoise-to-blue ramp in the order they were recorded.
  • The publication stays; the artwork changes. Masthead, frame, type, palette and devices are constant. Each issue gets entirely new art.
  • One loud thing per screen. Artwork carries the energy. Everything around it is calm, gridded and readable.
  • Print logic, not glow. Flat inks, halftone, cut-outs and grain. No gradients for decoration, no neon glows.

Artwork

What stays, what changes

  • Constant in every issue: masthead at the top, slug line, ink frame, cover-line strip, typography, palette, echo device, halftone screen and grain.
  • New in every issue: the subject, the field shapes, and which device leads (an orbit, a burst, a stack of echoes). The issue 07 listener is that issue’s art only; it is never a logo or mascot.

Devices

  • Planes. Field, then echoes, then masthead, then subject, then foreground details, then text. A cover should show at least three planes at once.
  • Echoes. A silhouette or glyph repeated in the version ramp, offset in one direction. The nearest echo is the first cover. Echoes recede toward the background color.
  • Halftone. A 45° dot screen whose dots grow away from one light source. Texture belongs on the subject; echoes and fields stay flat.
  • Cut-out. A paper-colored edge and a hard offset shadow lift the subject off its echoes.
  • Grain. Soft-light noise at about 30% on ink, multiply on paper.
  • Generated sleeves. When a recording has no image, its sleeve is a halftone in its version color with its year. Nothing waits for artist photography.

Never

  • Text over busy artwork, or artwork under controls.
  • Fabricated photographs of real artists, or copied artwork.
  • More than one loud artwork in the same screen.

Layout and spacing

  • Grid: 12 columns, gutter clamp(1rem, 1.7vw, 1.5rem), margins clamp(1.25rem, 3.3vw, 3rem).
  • Spacing scale: 4, 8, 12, 16, 24, 32, 48, 64, 96 px.
  • Cover: the full first screen below the bar. Masthead row, slug, story in columns 1–6, cover lines in columns 1–6; artwork on the right, bleeding off the edge.
  • Interior: section title in columns 1–6 with the intro in 7–11. Cards are asymmetric (7 and 4 columns), the smaller one offset down by 96px.
  • Listening: the original in columns 1–4 and the family tree in 5–12, with a sticky player below.
  • Between ink and paper: a halftone dissolve band (a 12px screen over 10 rows; 10px on phones), never a hard edge.
  • Breakpoints: up to 1100px, cards stack and the original sits above its covers, with smaller cover art. Up to 720px, the phone layout is a composition of its own: menu sheet, art panel, full-width actions, one-line idle player, swipeable back issues.

Motion

  • One entrance per page: wordmark letters flip from B-side to A-side, echoes slide out from behind the subject, the story text rises. About 1.6 seconds in all.
  • Responses over decoration: the main action fans the echoes; letters flip on hover; each card’s art reacts to its own hover. Play, pause and compare respond immediately.
  • Ambient: only the satellite moves (a 16-second lap) and it stops when the cover is off screen.
  • Depth: pointer parallax for mouse users; gentle scroll depth on the cover.
  • Timing: 150ms for state, 300ms for hover, 600–900ms for expressive moves. Easing cubic-bezier(0.2, 0.8, 0.2, 1).
  • Reduced motion: no entrance, parallax, orbit or wipes. Everything appears in its final state.

Accessibility

  • Contrast: every text pair in the table above meets WCAG AA (4.5:1); display type is far above 3:1.
  • Focus: a visible 3px outline, pink on ink and link-blue on paper.
  • Targets: at least 44 × 44px; the primary actions are 54px tall.
  • Artwork: decorative and hidden from assistive technology, with its meaning always also in text (“one original from 1998 and the five covers it inspired”).
  • Text and art: text never sits on busy artwork. On phones the artwork gets its own panel, clear of the masthead and the story.
  • Player: buttons say what they play and how long it is, expose their pressed state, and a live region announces what is playing.
  • Language: non-English titles carry a lang attribute.
  • Navigation: the phone menu is a real button with aria-expanded; focus moves into the sheet and back, and Escape closes it.
